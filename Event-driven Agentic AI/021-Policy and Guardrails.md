### Policy and Guardrails

Agents are powerful and sometimes are too powerful to be saved by default, guardrails make them safe, explainable, and auditable. The first type of a guardrail is the content filter. You don't want any users to send any prompts to your agents, your agents need to be specialized and do only what they were designed to do. Firstly, LLM tokens can be expensive if overused, secondly, allowing anything is a security risk. Content filters ensure that irrelevant requests are rejected. There are also approval gates. For example, if you process refunds, you don't want to get your team members bombarded with approval requests, or to approve anything below a certain amount., only get a human involved if the amount is greater than that. Any types of guardrails come with decision logs, these record what decisions have been made and why, very useful if you want to ensure that your agent's guardrails work as intended. You can see whether certain prompts can still bypass your guardrails or whether your guardrails are so strict that your agents can no longer do their normal jobs.
