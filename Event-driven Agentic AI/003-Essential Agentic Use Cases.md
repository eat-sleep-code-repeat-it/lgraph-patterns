### Essential Agentic Use Cases
I will give you some examples of the core use cases for agentic event‑driven systems. You will look at monitoring, triage, approvals, RAG refresh, commerce fraud, and ops runbooks.

Event‑driven agentic AI systems are extremely useful in automated monitoring, this is your smoke detector. Agents subscribe to health and business streams, watch performance of your system's components, and raise alerts when thresholds trip. Like a detector, they don't just beep, they identify the room and suggest the nearest extinguisher. They show you the suspected cause of the problem, estimate its likely blast radius, and suggest the first actions you would need to take to fix it. Because they're intelligent, in some cases they can also fix the problem without any human involvement. 

A closely‑related use case for event‑driven AI systems is incident triage, this process is the ER nurse of operations. Events arrive, which indicates that there may be a problem with your system, such as timeouts, users spikes, or errors. The agent categorizes the symptoms, pulls patient history, logs recent deploys, assigns severity, and routes to the right specialist, which may be another agent or a human in the loop. 

Agents are also used for automated approvals in your workflows, they act as your airport gate security, they verify identity, apply rules, escalate exceptions, and keep the line moving. 

Event‑driven agents run multi‑step workflows, they collect context, request human sign‑off when needed, set timers, and auto advance or rollback if the window expires. 

Large language models, or LLMs, often rely on technology known as retrieval‑augmented generation, or RAG, for storing this small business data, such as private companies' documents. Documents need to be updated, and the RAG data needs to be refreshed, this can be done by event‑driven agentic systems. 

RAG refresh is a librarian with a news wire, as sources such as documents, tickets, pull requests, etc., change, the agents ingest the differences via webhook and/or CDC. It then updates the context in the RAG datastore, rebuilding indexes so the data becomes searchable again, and validates retrieval quality so the data remains usable. Your knowledge stays current without you having to lift a finger. 

Agents can monitor your business processes. Commerce signals are a store detective looking out for fraud patterns, such as sudden refunds, mismatched geographic locations or IP addresses, rapid gift card purchases, and so on. 

Agents score risk in real time, request extra verification, or hold fulfillment. Some events can also drive upsell or cross sell other items by making highly‑personalized offers when the user's intent to buy is at its strongest. 

Agentic systems can automate and simplify important IT operations. When your disk gets full or a certificate expires, the agents execute the appropriate playbook, they gather diagnostics, run safe commands, verify outcomes, and document every step for audit. If a step fails, the agents orchestrate a compensative action, bringing the system back into the last‑known good state. 

There are many more use cases for agentic systems, but they follow similar patterns, they react to events in real time and perform tasks that until recently only humans could do.
